# Technician Class (Element 2) Study Guides

Ten guides, one per subelement, covering the **NCVEC 2026–2030 Technician Class question pool** —
effective July 1, 2026 through June 30, 2030, current through the **errata issued February 19,
2026**.

**Every one of the 409 pool questions appears in these guides**, printed in full with all four
choices, the correct answer marked, and the Part 97 citation where the pool provides one. Each
group's questions sit directly beneath the concept notes that explain them.

**The exam:** 35 questions, one drawn at random from each of the 35 groups. **You need 26 correct
to pass (74%).** You can miss 9.

## Where to spend your effort

**T1 alone is 6 of your 35 questions** — 17% of the exam — and it's almost pure memorization with
no math and no diagrams, which makes it the single best return on study time in the pool. **T5,
T6, T7, and T8 add another 16 questions** between them, forming the technical core of the exam:
units and Ohm's Law, component vocabulary, station equipment and troubleshooting, and modulation
modes. T1 plus that technical core is 22 of your 35 questions — most of the way to the 26 you need
to pass.

A reasonable order:

1. **T1** — frontload the rules tables while you're fresh; six questions for the price of reading
   one document carefully
2. **T0** — safety facts, straight recall, and genuinely worth knowing cold rather than just for
   test day
3. **T5** — the units, metric-prefix, decibel, and Ohm's Law foundation that T6, T7, and T8 all
   lean on
4. **T6** — component vocabulary, builds directly on T5 (resistor, capacitor, inductor, diode,
   transistor, schematic symbols)
5. **T7** — station equipment, troubleshooting, and test instruments, builds on T6
6. **T8** — modulation modes, satellites, and digital-mode vocabulary; dense but pure recall
7. **T2** — operating procedures, concrete and easy to picture (offsets, calling frequencies, nets)
8. **T3** — propagation modes, mostly vocabulary matched to a one-line signature
9. **T4, T9** — small; save for last, review right before the exam

## Things to actually memorize

Everything else you can reason out. These you can't:

**Numbers**
- Repeater offsets: 2 m is **±600 kHz**; 70 cm is **±5 MHz**
- National FM simplex calling frequency: **146.520 MHz** (2 m)
- Technician HF phone: **28.300–28.500 MHz only**; power there capped at **200 W PEP**
- Above 30 MHz, power jumps to **1500 W PEP**
- CW-only slivers: **50.0–50.1 MHz** and **144.0–144.1 MHz**
- Automatically controlled HF beacons: **28.200–28.300 MHz only**
- License term **10 years**; renew up to **90 days** early; **2-year** grace period (renew, but
  cannot transmit, during it)
- Club station license needs **at least 4 members**
- Identify with your FCC call sign at least **every 10 minutes**
- Space station: located **more than 50 km** above Earth's surface
- SWR meter: **1:1** is a perfect match, **4:1** is a mismatch
- Dummy load: **50-ohm non-inductive resistor** on a heat sink
- Bandwidth order, narrowest to widest: **CW ~150 Hz → SSB ~3 kHz → FM voice 10–15 kHz → fast-scan
  TV ~6 MHz**
- LEO satellites: period of about **100 minutes**
- **50 MHz** has the lowest maximum permissible RF exposure of the tested bands
- Antenna-to-power-line clearance: enough that a fallen antenna can't come within **10 feet**
- Tower grounding: **separate 8-foot ground rods per leg**, bonded together
- A 19-inch vertical on 2 m is a resonant **quarter-wave**
- **Type N** connectors for above 400 MHz; **50 ohms** is the standard coax impedance

**Formulas**
- `I = E/R`, `E = I×R`, `R = E/I` (Ohm's Law)
- `P = I × E` (DC power)
- `+3 dB` = double power, `+10 dB` = 10× power; halvings stack (two halvings = **-6 dB**)
- Metric ladder: **milli ×0.001, kilo ×1000, mega ×1,000,000, micro ×0.000001, pico ×10⁻¹²**
- `wavelength (m) = 300 ÷ frequency (MHz)`
- Battery runtime = amp-hour rating ÷ average current draw

**Conventions with no logic behind them**
- Black wire insulation means **hot** in US 120 V AC wiring
- Fuses and breakers go **in series with the hot conductor only**, never the neutral
- A diode's cathode lead is marked with a **stripe**
- Upper sideband is used on 10 m, VHF, and UHF SSB
- A Technician's Group D call sign format is a two-letter prefix, one digit, three-letter suffix
  (e.g., KF1XXX)

## Traps that catch people

- **A 12-volt battery's real hazard is shorting the terminals, not touching both terminals** — 12
  V is too low to shock you that way. (T0A01)
- **The renewal grace period protects your right to renew, not your right to operate** — you may
  not transmit on an expired license even while it's within the 2-year grace period. (T1C09/T1C11)
- **A Technician may never operate in an Amateur Extra band segment, even if "designated" to do so
  by an Extra licensee** — outside an emergency, at no time. (T1E06)
- **Technician HF power (200 W) is a fraction of General's 1500 W on the same 10-meter band** —
  don't assume the higher VHF/UHF cap applies to the HF phone segment. (T1B11/T1B12)
- **Don't swap the offsets:** 2 meters is ±600 kHz, 70 centimeters is ±5 MHz. (T2A01/T2A03)
- **F-region skip on 10 meters is a daytime phenomenon** — best from dawn to shortly after sunset
  during high sunspot activity, not overnight. (T3C09)
- **2400 Hz, not the narrowest filter, gives the best SNR for SSB reception** — narrow enough to
  cut noise, wide enough not to clip the voice audio SSB needs. (T4B10)
- **Two successive halvings of power stack to -6 dB, not -3 dB.** (T5B10)
- **Solid-state transmitters cut power at high SWR to protect their own output transistors, not to
  comply with an FCC spectral-purity rule.** (T7C05)
- **FM's capture effect means only one signal can be received at a time** — unlike SSB, where
  overlapping signals can often still be picked apart. (T8A12)
- **A safety wire through a turnbuckle stops the turnbuckle from loosening under vibration** — it
  is not a backup connection and carries no lightning current. (T0B05)

## Sources and currency

Questions are reproduced verbatim from the NCVEC 2026–2030 Element 2 pool, which the NCVEC
Question Pool Committee **released into the public domain**. This pool replaced the 2022–2026
Technician pool and took effect July 1, 2026.

**Errata applied.** Four questions received minor wording clarifications before the pool even went
into effect, via the errata issued February 19, 2026: **T1C01, T5A05, T7A09, T0A10**. Those
clarified versions are what's reflected in these guides.

**Unlike the General and Extra pools, this pool has no withdrawn or deleted questions** —
numbering is fully contiguous from T0A01 through T9B12.

The current pool expires **June 30, 2030**. After that date, check NCVEC for the replacement.

**A study guide is not a substitute for practice tests.** These explain the material so the
answers stick, and having the full pool in front of you means you can self-test as you go. But
running full randomized 35-question mock exams is what tells you whether you're ready. Aim for
consistent scores in the mid-80s before booking a session — that leaves margin for exam-day
nerves. This is a first license, and the material here is intentionally lighter than General or
Extra: no math beyond basic Ohm's Law and simple decibel conversions, oriented toward VHF/UHF FM
operating, station setup, and safety.
