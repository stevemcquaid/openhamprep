algebra: unit terminology (current, voltage, power, frequency), metric-prefix conversions and
decibels, the definitions behind capacitance/inductance/impedance, and Ohm's Law with series and

memorization — but it rewards that effort directly, since General class licensees will recognize this
same material (units, decibels, Ohm's Law) return in more depth in G5.

## T5A — Current and voltage: terminology and units, conductors and insulators, alternating and direct current

*One exam question comes from this group. 11 questions in the pool.*

**The core unit vocabulary, asked from multiple directions:** current is measured in **amperes**,
power in **watts**, and frequency in **hertz**. *Current* is the term for the **flow of electrons** in
a circuit, while a **difference in voltage** is what causes that electron flow in the first place —
voltage is the "pressure," current is the resulting "flow." *Power* is the term for the **rate at
which electrical energy is used**, distinct from energy itself. *Frequency* is the number of complete
cycles an alternating current makes **per second**, and its unit is the **hertz**.

**Conductors vs. insulators comes down to free electrons.** Metals are good conductors because they
have **many free electrons** available to carry current; glass is a good insulator because it does
not. (Sea water, stainless steel, and graphite are all distractors offered as insulators — they are
actually conductors, some poor, but conductors nonetheless.)

**Alternating current** is defined as current that **alternates between positive and negative
directions** — not merely between a direction and zero, which is how the wrong answers try to trick
you. **Resistance opposes current flow regardless of type**: direct current, alternating current, and
RF current are all opposed by resistance — "all these choices are correct" for that one.

## T5B — Math for electronics: conversion of electrical units, decibels

*One exam question comes from this group. 13 questions in the pool.*

**Metric prefix conversions make up most of this group.** Keep the ladder straight: **milli-** is
one-thousandth (x0.001), **kilo-** is one thousand (x1000), **mega-** is one million (x1,000,000),
**giga-** is one billion, **micro-** is one-millionth, and **pico-** is one-trillionth. Working through
the pool's examples: 1.5 amperes is **1500 milliamperes** (amps to milliamps: multiply by 1000); 3000
milliamperes is **3 amperes** (milliamps to amps: divide by 1000); one kilovolt is **1000 volts**; one
microvolt is **one one-millionth of a volt**; 500 milliwatts is **0.5 watts**; 1,000,000 picofarads is
**1 microfarad** (pico to micro is six orders of magnitude, same as milli-to-kilo run in reverse).

**Frequency conversions follow the same ladder, just applied to hertz.** 1,500,000 hertz equals
**1500 kHz** (divide by 1000 to go Hz→kHz); 3.525 MHz equals **3525 kHz** (multiply by 1000 to go
MHz→kHz); 28400 kHz equals **28.400 MHz** (divide by 1000 to go kHz→MHz); 2425 MHz equals **2.425 GHz**
(divide by 1000 to go MHz→GHz).

**Decibels measure a power ratio, and the pool sticks to three landmark values:** doubling power is
**+3 dB**, a tenfold increase is **+10 dB**, and each halving subtracts another 3 dB. So 5 watts to 10
watts (a doubling) is **+3 dB**; 20 watts to 200 watts (a tenfold increase) is **+10 dB**; and 12 watts
down to 3 watts is *two* successive halvings (12→6→3), so **-3 dB** twice stacks to **-6 dB**.

## T5C — Capacitance and inductance terminology and units; Radio frequency definition and units; Impedance definition and units; Calculating power

*One exam question comes from this group. 12 questions in the pool.*

**Two component definitions, mirror images of each other.** *Capacitance* is the ability to **store
energy in an electric field**, and its unit is the **farad**. *Inductance* is the ability to **store
energy in a magnetic field**, and its unit is the **henry**. Keep the pairing straight: electric field
↔ capacitance ↔ farad; magnetic field ↔ inductance ↔ henry.

**Impedance** is defined here simply as **the opposition to AC current flow**, and its unit is the
**ohm** — the same unit as plain resistance, since impedance is resistance's AC generalization.

**Frequency abbreviations are case-sensitive on this exam.** The correct abbreviation for kilohertz is
**kHz** (lowercase k, capital H, lowercase z); for megahertz it's **MHz** (capital M). The
lowercase/uppercase distractors (khz, KHz, KHZ, mHz, mHZ, Mhz) are all wrong.

**Power in a DC circuit** uses the simplest form of the power law: **P = I x E** (current times
voltage). Two worked examples from the pool: 13.8 volts DC at 10 amperes delivers 13.8 x 10 =
**138 watts**; 12 volts DC at 2.5 amperes delivers 12 x 2.5 = **30 watts**. Rearranged to solve for
current, **I = P / E**: delivering 120 watts at 12 volts DC requires 120 / 12 = **10 amperes**.

## T5D — Ohm's Law; Series and parallel circuits

*One exam question comes from this group. 14 questions in the pool.*

**Ohm's Law, three forms of the same relationship** — pick whichever solves for the value you're
missing:

```
I = E / R          E = I x R          R = E / I
```

**Worked resistance examples:** 3 amperes through 90 volts gives R = 90 / 3 = **30 ohms**; 1.5 amperes
at 12 volts gives R = 12 / 1.5 = **8 ohms**; 4 amperes from a 12-volt source gives R = 12 / 4 =
**3 ohms**.

**Worked current examples:** 120 volts across 80 ohms gives I = 120 / 80 = **1.5 amperes**; 200 volts
across 100 ohms gives I = 200 / 100 = **2 amperes**; 240 volts across 24 ohms gives I = 240 / 24 =
**10 amperes**.

**Worked voltage examples:** 0.5 amperes through 2 ohms gives E = 0.5 x 2 = **1 volt**; 1 ampere
through 10 ohms gives E = 1 x 10 = **10 volts**; 2 amperes through 10 ohms gives E = 2 x 10 =
**20 volts**.

**Series vs. parallel, the one fact each:** in a **series** circuit, the **current is the same through
all components** — there's only one path, so every component sees the same flow. In a **parallel**
circuit, the **voltage is the same across all components** — every branch connects across the same two
nodes, so every branch sees the same potential difference.

## Bottom line for T5

Current is **amperes**, power is **watts**, frequency is **hertz**; a **voltage difference** drives
**current** (electron flow), and **power** is the rate energy is used. AC **alternates between
positive and negative directions**. Metals conduct because of **free electrons**; glass insulates.
**Resistance opposes DC, AC, and RF current alike.**

Metric prefixes: **milli x0.001, kilo x1000, mega x1,000,000, micro x0.000001, pico x10^-12.**
Decibels: **+3 dB = double power, +10 dB = 10x power**, and halvings stack (two halvings = **-6 dB**).

**Capacitance** stores energy in an **electric field** (unit: **farad**); **inductance** stores energy
in a **magnetic field** (unit: **henry**); **impedance** is opposition to AC current flow (unit:
**ohm**). Abbreviations are case-sensitive: **kHz**, **MHz**. DC power: **P = I x E**.

Ohm's Law: **I = E/R, E = I x R, R = E/I**. **Series circuits share the same current**; **parallel
circuits share the same voltage**.
