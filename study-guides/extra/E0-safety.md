## E0A — Safety: RF radiation hazards; hazardous materials; grounding

*One exam question comes from this group. 12 questions in the pool.*

**RF exposure basics.** The FCC's human body RF exposure limits are **most restrictive from 30-300
MHz** — the range where a standing adult body is roughly resonant and absorbs energy most
efficiently. **SAR** (Specific Absorption Rate) measures **the rate at which RF energy is absorbed
by the body**. There are separate electric (E) field and magnetic (H) field MPE limits below 300
MHz because the body reacts to both fields, ground reflections and scattering make field strength
vary with location, and the E- and H-field intensity peaks can occur at different spots — **all
these choices are correct**.

**Controlled vs. uncontrolled.** When your own signals reach a **neighbor's** property, that
neighbor is in an *uncontrolled* environment (someone with no awareness of or control over the
exposure), so you must keep signal levels below the tighter **uncontrolled MPE limits** — not the
looser controlled limits that apply to you and people who know about and can control their own
exposure. Watch the distractor pairs here: "controlled" vs. "uncontrolled," and "exposure" vs.
"emission" (MPE is always about *exposure*, never "emission").

**Multiple transmitters at one site.** Each transmitter's operator and licensee is responsible for
fixing an over-exposure situation only if that transmitter contributes **5 percent or more of its
own MPE limit** in an area where the combined total exceeds the limit — a low bar, so a lot of
co-located stations end up sharing responsibility.

**Microwave-specific hazard.** Microwaves aren't ionizing and don't travel faster — the real hazard
is that the **high-gain antennas commonly used at microwave frequencies concentrate energy**,
producing higher exposure levels than the same transmitter power would at lower frequencies with a
less directional antenna.

**Who is exempt from RF exposure evaluation.** Handheld transceivers **sold before May 3, 2021**
carry a grandfathered exemption. Power-level thresholds and near-field-only antennas are
distractors — they are not how the exemption works. On the flip side, for a station like 80 meters,
an evaluation **must always be performed** — there's no automatic pass based on power or mode alone.

**Grounding.** The primary purpose of an external earth ground rod is **lightning charge
dissipation** — it is a safety ground, not a way to cut RF current between equipment (that's
station bonding, a different topic) and not surge protection for a breaker panel.

**Tower and climbing safety.** **"100% tie-off"** means **at least one lanyard is attached to the
tower at all times** — as one lanyard releases to reposition, the other stays connected, so the
climber is never fully detached. Lanyards clip to the **tower legs** (not the antenna mast, guy
brackets, or rungs — legs are the structural member rated for a fall). A **shock-absorbing lanyard**
should be attached **above the climber's head level**, so a fall is arrested before much slack can
develop, minimizing fall distance and impact force.

#### All 12 pool questions for E0A

**E0A01**

What is the primary function of an external earth connection or ground rod?

- A. Prevent static build up on power lines
- B. Lightning charge dissipation
- C. Reduce RF current flow between pieces of equipment
- D. Protect breaker panel from power surges
- Answer: B

**E0A02**

When evaluating RF exposure levels from your station at a neighbor’s home, what must you do?

- A. Ensure signals from your station are less than the controlled maximum permissible exposure (MPE) limits
- B. Ensure signals from your station are less than the uncontrolled maximum permissible exposure (MPE) limits
- C. Ensure signals from your station are less than the controlled maximum permissible emission (MPE) limits
- D. Ensure signals from your station are less than the uncontrolled maximum permissible emission (MPE) limits
- Answer: B

**E0A03**

Over what range of frequencies are the FCC human body RF exposure limits most restrictive?

- A. 300 kHz - 3 MHz
- B. 3 - 30 MHz
- C. 30 - 300 MHz
- D. 300 - 3000 MHz
- Answer: C

**E0A04**

When evaluating a site with multiple transmitters operating at the same time, the operators and licensees of which transmitters are responsible for mitigating over-exposure situations?

- A. Each transmitter that produces 20 percent or more of its MPE limit in areas where the total MPE limit is exceeded
- B. Each transmitter operating with a duty cycle greater than 25 percent
- C. Each transmitter that produces 5 percent or more of its MPE limit in areas where the total MPE limit is exceeded
- D. Each transmitter operating with a duty cycle greater than 50 percent
- Answer: C

**E0A05**

What hazard is created by operating at microwave frequencies?

- A. Microwaves are ionizing radiation
- B. The high gain antennas commonly used can result in high exposure levels
- C. Microwaves are in the frequency range where wave velocity is higher
- D. The extremely high frequency energy can damage the joints of antenna structures
- Answer: B

**E0A06**

Why are there separate electric (E) and magnetic (H) MPE limits at frequencies below 300 MHz?

- A. The body reacts to electromagnetic radiation from both the E and H fields
- B. Ground reflections and scattering cause the field strength to vary with location
- C. E field and H field radiation intensity peaks can occur at different locations
- D. All these choices are correct
- Answer: D

**E0A07**

What is meant by “100% tie-off” regarding tower safety?

- A. All loose ropes and guys secured to a fixed structure
- B. At least one lanyard attached to the tower at all times
- C. All tools secured to the climber’s harness
- D. All circuit breakers feeding power to the tower must be tied closed with tape, cable, or ties
- Answer: B

**E0A08**

What does SAR measure?

- A. Signal attenuation ratio
- B. Signal amplification rating
- C. The rate at which RF energy is absorbed by the body
- D. The rate of RF energy reflected from stationary terrain
- Answer: C

**E0A09**

Which of the following types of equipment are exempt from RF exposure evaluations?

- A. Transceivers with less than 7 watts of RF output
- B. Antennas that radiate only in the near field
- C. Hand-held transceivers sold before May 3, 2021
- D. Dish antennas less than one meter in diameter
- Answer: C

**E0A10**

When must an RF exposure evaluation be performed on an amateur station operating on 80 meters?

- A. An evaluation must always be performed
- B. When the ERP of the station is less than 10 watts
- C. When the station’s operating mode is CW
- D. When the output power from the transmitter is less than 100 watts
- Answer: A

**E0A11**

To what should lanyards be attached while climbing?

- A. Antenna mast
- B. Guy brackets
- C. Tower rungs
- D. Tower legs
- Answer: D

**E0A12**

Where should a shock-absorbing lanyard be attached to a tower when working above ground?

- A. Above the climber’s head level
- B. To the belt of the fall-arrest harness
- C. Even with the climber's waist
- D. To the next lowest set of guys
- Answer: A
