# General Class (Element 3) Study Guides

Ten guides, one per subelement, covering the **NCVEC 2023–2027 General Class question pool** — current through the **6th errata of February 4, 2026**.

**Every one of the 423 active pool questions appears in these guides**, printed in full with all four choices, the correct answer marked, and the Part 97 citation where the pool provides one. Each group's questions sit directly beneath the concept notes that explain them.

**The exam:** 35 questions, one drawn at random from each of the 35 groups. **You need 26 correct to pass (74%).** You can miss 9.

## Where to spend your effort

**G1, G2, and G4 are 15 of your 35 questions** — 43% of the exam. G1 in particular is nearly pure memorization with no reasoning required, which makes it the best return on study time anywhere in the pool. Add **G9** and those four subelements cover 19 of the 35 you need, against a passing bar of 26.

A reasonable order:

1. **G1** — frontload the rules tables while you're fresh
2. **G2** — Q signals and digital mode facts, mostly flashcard material
3. **G4** — station practices, very concrete
4. **G9** — three formulas plus antenna behavior
5. **G3** — build the ionosphere model once and it sticks
6. **G5** — do the math problems by hand at least once
7. **G7, G8** — definitions and two formulas
8. **G6, G0** — small; save for last, review right before the exam

## Things to actually memorize

Everything else you can reason out. These you can't:

**Numbers**
- 468 ÷ MHz = half-wave dipole in feet; 234 ÷ MHz = quarter-wave
- 200 W PEP on 30 m; 1500 W nearly everywhere else
- 1 S unit = 6 dB; 3 dB = 2× power; 1 dB loss = 20.6%
- dBi = dBd + 2.15
- E region hop ≈ 1,200 miles; F2 hop ≈ 2,500 miles
- Solar flare 8 minutes; CME 15 hours to days; solar rotation 26–28 days
- Solar flux measured at 10.7 cm
- CSCE valid 365 days; VE minimum age 18; 3 VEs per exam
- Silicon diode 0.7 V; germanium 0.3 V; lead-acid floor 10.5 V
- AWG 12 for 20 A; AWG 14 takes a 15 A fuse
- Spread spectrum 10 W PEP; beacons 100 W PEP

**Formulas**
- `P = V×I`, `P = V²/R`, `P = I²R`
- `V_RMS = V_peak × 0.707`
- PEP from peak-to-peak: halve, ×0.707, square, divide by R
- `SWR = larger Z ÷ smaller Z`
- FM bandwidth = `2 × (deviation + modulating frequency)`
- Transformer voltage follows turns; impedance follows turns **squared**
- Capacitors combine backwards from resistors and inductors

**Conventions with no logic behind them**
- LSB below 10 MHz, USB above — because that's the custom
- RTTY AFSK uses LSB; everything else modern uses USB
- General gets the *upper* portion of a shared voice segment
- The four bands with Extra-only segments: 80, 40, 20, 15

## Traps that catch people

- **A tuner at the shack does not change the SWR on the feed line.** (G9A08)
- **Feed line loss makes measured SWR look *better*, not worse.** (G9A11)
- **K-index is short-term, A-index is long-term** — both measure *Earth's* field. (G3A12/13)
- **Critical frequency and critical angle are both the *highest*** value that returns a wave. (G3C02/04)
- **A speech processor raises average power, not peak power.** (G4D02)
- **PEP equals average power for an unmodulated carrier** — ratio 1.00. (G5B11)
- **Odd-order IMD products are the close-in ones.** Add the coefficients to check order. (G8B05/13)
- **Class C works for FM only**, never SSB or AM. (G7B11)
- **A General VE may administer Technician exams only.** (G1D02)
- **Remote operation follows the law of wherever the transmitter is.** (G1D05 vs G1D12)

## Sources and currency

Questions are reproduced verbatim from the NCVEC 2023–2027 Element 3 pool, which the NCVEC Question Pool Committee **released into the public domain**. Answer keys were spot-checked against the official NCVEC release document.

**Errata applied.** Eight questions have been withdrawn since the original 2022 release and are correctly absent here: **G1A04, G1C08, G1C09, G1C10, G1E09, G6B09, G8C01, G9C06**. Several older study guides, practice apps, and PDFs still include them — notably the 300/1200 baud symbol-rate questions and the 60-meter ERP question in G1C, which are no longer testable. Question numbering in the pool is therefore not perfectly contiguous; that's expected, not an error.

The current pool expires **June 30, 2027**. After that date, check NCVEC for the replacement.

**A study guide is not a substitute for practice tests.** These explain the material so the answers stick, and having the full pool in front of you means you can self-test as you go. But running full randomized 35-question mock exams is what tells you whether you're ready. Aim for consistent scores in the mid-80s before booking a session — that leaves margin for exam-day nerves.
