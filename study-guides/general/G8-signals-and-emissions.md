## G8A — Carriers and modulation; modulation envelope; overmodulation; link budgets

*One exam question comes from this group. 14 questions in the pool.*

**A carrier has exactly three properties you can vary, and each gives a modulation type:**

| Vary | Mode | How the exam phrases it |
|---|---|---|
| Amplitude | **AM** | "varies the instantaneous **power level**" |
| Frequency | **FM** | "changes the instantaneous **frequency**" |
| Phase | **PM** | "changes the **phase angle**" |

A **reactance modulator connected to an RF amplifier stage produces phase modulation** — the same
modulator applied to the oscillator would give FM, but downstream at the amplifier it is PM.

**Digital modulation.** Direct binary FSK is generated by **changing an oscillator's frequency
directly with a digital control signal**. **FT8 uses 8-tone FSK**. **QPSK** sends data using **0, 90,
180, and 270 degree phase shifts to represent pairs of bits** — quadrature means four phases, so the
name gives you the answer.

**Bandwidth and distortion.** The **narrowest phone emission is single sideband**. **Overmodulation
causes excessive bandwidth** (splatter). **"Flat-topping"** is distortion from **excessive drive or
speech level** — the peaks get clipped flat. The **modulation envelope** is the waveform created by
**connecting the peak values** of the modulated signal.

**Budget versus margin.** A **link budget** is **transmit power plus antenna gains minus system
losses, as seen at the receiver** — note that it *includes* transmit power. **Link margin** is the
**difference between received power and the minimum required signal level**. Budget is what arrives;
margin is what is left over.

## G8B — Frequency changing; bandwidths of various modes; deviation; intermodulation

*One exam question comes from this group. 13 questions in the pool.*

**Mixing.** Another term for mixing two RF signals is **heterodyning**, and the output contains the
**sum and difference** of the inputs. The **local oscillator** is the input you tune, because the IF
stage is fixed. **Image response** is interference from a signal **twice the IF away** — since a
mixer produces both sum and difference, two different input frequencies can land on the same IF, and
the unwanted one is the image. A **multiplier** stage generates a **harmonic of a lower frequency
signal** to reach a VHF operating frequency.

**Intermodulation** is what happens when **two signals combine in a non-linear circuit to produce
unwanted spurious outputs**. **Odd-order products are closest to the original frequencies**, which is
exactly why they are the troublesome ones — even-order products land far away and filter out easily.
To check the order of a product, **add the coefficients**: 2F1 - F2 gives 2+1 = 3, odd.

**FM bandwidth:** `2 x (deviation + highest modulating frequency)`. So 5 kHz deviation with 3 kHz
audio gives 2 x 8 = **16 kHz**.

**Deviation through a multiplier** scales by the same factor as the frequency. Find the factor first:
146.52 / 12.21 = 12, so 5,000 Hz / 12 = **416.7 Hz** at the oscillator.

**Bandwidth and rate.** **Higher symbol rates need wider bandwidth.** Match receiver bandwidth to the
mode for the **best signal-to-noise ratio** — extra bandwidth only admits extra noise. And know your
**duty cycle**, because high-duty-cycle modes **could exceed the transmitter's average power rating**.
That is why you reduce power on FT8 and RTTY.

## G8C — Digital emission modes

*One exam question comes from this group. 15 questions in the pool.*

**Mode identification.** **WSPR** is the **low-power beacon for assessing HF propagation**. **FT8**
is the one that **decodes at very low signal-to-noise ratios**. **PSK31** uses **Varicode**, in which
**upper case letters use longer sequences and slow transmission down** — type in capitals and you
literally transmit slower. **Baudot** is a **5-bit code with start and stop bits**. The **digital
voice** modes are **DMR, D-STAR, and System Fusion**.

**Error control — two different philosophies.** **ARQ** asks for a resend when something arrives
broken: a **NAK requests retransmission**, and too many failed attempts means **the connection is
dropped**. **FEC** instead **transmits redundant information with the data** so the receiver can
repair errors without asking.

**Vocabulary.** A packet frame's **header** carries routing and handling information. An FSK signal's
two frequencies are **mark and space**. In a **mesh**, if one node fails **a packet may still reach
its target via an alternate node** — redundant routing is the whole point.

**Waterfall displays** show **frequency horizontally, signal strength as intensity, and time
vertically**. **Vertical lines on either side of a data signal mean overmodulation** — that is
splatter, so turn the drive down. An **FT8 report of +3** means the **signal-to-noise ratio is
equivalent to +3 dB in a 2.5 kHz bandwidth**, which is why a report of -15 is normal and still
perfectly decodable.

## Bottom line for G8

**AM varies amplitude, FM frequency, PM phase** — and a reactance modulator on an amplifier gives
**PM**. FT8 is **8-tone FSK**; QPSK uses **four phases for pairs of bits**. **SSB is the narrowest
phone mode**; overmodulation causes **excessive bandwidth**. Mixing is **heterodyning** and yields
**sum and difference**; the image sits **twice the IF away**; **odd-order** products are closest in.
FM bandwidth is **2 x (deviation + audio)**. **WSPR** beacons, **PSK31** uses **Varicode**, **NAK**
means resend, **FEC** adds redundancy, FSK has **mark and space**.
